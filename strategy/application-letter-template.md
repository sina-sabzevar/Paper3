# Application letter — advertised European PhD positions

One page. Never two. A PI screening 40 applications for a salaried position gives each one
about ninety seconds, and the question they are answering is *"can this person do the project
I described?"* — not *"is this person impressive?"*

That is the whole difference from a US statement of purpose, and why your American-style
material has been failing in this market.

---

## Structure

Five paragraphs, roughly 320–400 words total.

| ¶ | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Which position, and who you are in one line | 2 sentences |
| 2 | **Their project → your specific, evidenced capability** | 4–6 sentences |
| 3 | Two things you bring that the ad did not ask for | 3–4 sentences |
| 4 | Where the evidence lives | 1–2 sentences |
| 5 | Practicalities: start date, relocation, language | 2 sentences |

**Paragraph 2 is the letter.** Everything else is packaging. It is also the only paragraph that
cannot be reused between applications — which is exactly why most applicants skip it, and why
writing it puts you ahead of most of the pile.

---

## Worked example

Against a real advertisement: a PhD position on *statistics and sample-size calculation for
fMRI research — simulation tools and power analysis for fMRI connectivity studies using
structural equation modelling.*

> Dear Dr **[Name]**,
>
> I am applying for the PhD position on power analysis and simulation tools for fMRI
> connectivity studies (ref. **[number]**). I work on causal discovery and effective
> connectivity in resting-state fMRI, and the sample-size problem this project addresses is the
> one that has constrained every study I have run.
>
> Your project builds simulation and power-analysis tools for SEM-based connectivity studies. My
> MSc and the two years following it were spent on the applied side of that same gap: I applied
> PC, GES and LiNGAM causal discovery to resting-state fMRI in schizophrenia (**[n]** patients,
> **[n]** controls) and estimated effective connectivity in a clinical epilepsy cohort
> (**[n]**), and in both the binding constraint was never the method but the number of subjects.
> That is what led to my current manuscript, which builds VAE- and GAN-based augmentation for
> connectivity data and evaluates it against SMOTE and cost-sensitive baselines
> (**[result]**). I have therefore spent real time on the question your project formalises —
> how much data a connectivity estimate needs before it can be trusted — approaching it as
> someone who kept hitting the wall rather than someone modelling it from outside.
>
> Two things I would bring beyond the advertised requirements. First, engineering scale: I
> currently build production ML pipelines over 70 million records and ran my fMRI analyses on
> HPC, so a simulation study needing thousands of synthetic datasets is infrastructure I have
> built before rather than would be learning. Second, a perspective from outside SEM:
> constraint-based and functional causal discovery rest on different assumptions, and a power
> analysis that holds across those families would be more useful than one tuned to a single
> estimator.
>
> Publications, code and figures are at **[site]**; the schizophrenia work appeared at ICSPIS
> 2023 and the epilepsy analysis at OHBM 2025.
>
> I can start **[date]**, I am ready to relocate, and I have worked in English throughout my MSc
> and current role (**[TOEFL, once retaken]**). I would be glad to discuss the project.
>
> Yours sincerely,
> Sina Sabzevar

Note what that letter never does: it does not narrate his career chronologically, does not
explain why he admires the university, and does not claim to be passionate about anything.
It argues one thing — *I have already been stuck on your problem* — and lets that carry.

## How to write one in twenty minutes

1. **Read the ad twice.** Underline only the sentences describing what the *work* is. Ignore the
   boilerplate about the graduate school and the salary scale.
2. **Write paragraph 2 first.** Name the project's actual technical content in your own words,
   then connect it to something you have done, with a number attached. If you cannot find a
   genuine connection, do not apply — that is a signal, not an obstacle to write around.
3. **Pick paragraph 3 from a fixed menu.** You have four differentiators; choose the two that
   fit: engineering scale and HPC; a complementary methods family (causal discovery vs.
   DCM/SEM/Granger); direct clinical collaboration experience; graduate-level teaching.
4. **Paragraphs 1, 4 and 5 are near-constant.** Keep them in a snippets file and edit only the
   position title, reference number and start date.
5. **Apply the reuse test.** Read the finished letter and ask: *could I send this to a different
   advertisement unchanged?* If yes, paragraph 2 has failed and the PI will see it immediately.

## Failure modes

Each of these is a visible tell that a letter is a template:

- "I am writing to express my interest in…" — three dead words before any content. Delete.
- A paragraph on why you admire the university, the city, or the country. Dutch and German PIs
  do not care and it reads as padding.
- Your CV retold in chronological order. They have the CV attached.
- Never naming the project's specific technical content. The single biggest tell.
- Over 450 words, or spilling onto a second page.
- An unrequested six-page research statement. Send what was asked for.
- "Dear Sir/Madam" when the advertisement names the supervisor.
- Claiming a skill the CV cannot evidence. These are salaried posts; the reference check is real.

## Mechanics

- **Subject line:** `Application — PhD position [ref number], [project short name]`
- **Attach:** the two-page CV, and the letter as a PDF if the portal takes uploads. Paste the
  same text as the email body when applying by email — do not make them open an attachment to
  learn who you are.
- **Transcripts and certificates** only when asked. Unrequested attachments slow a screener down.
- **Apply within a week of posting.** Many EU positions screen on a rolling basis and close
  when they find someone; early applications get read properly, late ones get skimmed.
- **Log every application** — date, institution, PI, reference, project, and which
  differentiator you used in ¶3. At 4–6 a month you will lose track by month two, and you need
  the record to tell a near-miss from a silent rejection.

## On the start date and the visa

EU PIs are hiring an employee, so the question behind their reading is partly practical: when
can this person actually begin, and will the paperwork be a problem? Address it plainly in one
sentence, as in the example, and move on. A confident, specific answer is reassuring. Silence
invites them to assume the worst, and a long explanation invites them to worry.
