Direct Carrier Billing · what it is and what it costs

Lending customers money on their next phone bill

A customer borrows against their phone bill, spends it on services they already use, and repays it on the bill that arrives next month. Every customer is scored, given a limit that matches their score, and the riskiest are declined. This is the size of that programme.

The revenue check does almost all the work. It removes 31,182,117 customers — 97 of every 100 we turn away. It is not a judgement about whether they would repay, only about whether their bill is large enough to carry a loan, so lowering it adds volume without making the book riskier. The score removes only 542,951 more, because by the time a customer has cleared the first three checks they are already a reasonable risk.

Risk per customer is not the same as risk to the book, and the limits are where that gets decided. The default rate column is the model's own score on the four-month history it was built from, used to order customers and set limits; the money below uses the one-month rate, because the product is one month.

One rule the limits have to respect

The loan lands on the customer's next bill. How far that bill jumps decides how likely they are to stop paying, and the effect is sharp.

Never lend someone more than they bill in a month.
Below that line the bill rises by less than double and customers carry on paying at close to the normal rate. At or above it the bill doubles — and customers whose bill doubles stop paying 1.7 times as often. The line sits exactly at "the loan equals the bill".

What it is likely to cost

Worked out in the open so every step can be argued with.

1

8,801,772 customers borrow 302,300 Toman on average

2,660.8 bn is out on loan at any one time. It comes back each month and goes out again, so 31,929 bn is lent across a full year.

2

The one-month default rate is 0.09 to 0.14%

The span is a choice about how wide to open the book, not an uncertainty. Lend only to the safest and it sits at the bottom; approve everyone the model passes and it sits at the top.

3

So we lose 28.7 to 44.7 bn Toman a year

That assumes we recover nothing from a customer who stops paying, which is deliberately pessimistic — the operator keeps collecting after the line is cut off. Against 31,929 bn lent, the midpoint is about 1.15 Toman lost for every 1,000 lent.

What we do not know yet

Two things, so nothing above is taken as more certain than it is.

Nobody in this data ever had credit

  • The score describes how customers behave when they are not borrowing. How they behave when they are is genuinely unknown.
  • A capped pilot is the only thing that answers it, and a one-month loan means the answer arrives in weeks rather than a year.

Is the 15,000 bn target lending or exposure?

  • At monthly turnover the two differ by twelve times. As lending over a year the programme reaches 31,929 bn, more than twice the target.
  • As money out at any one moment it reaches 2,660.8 bn and falls well short. Everything in the volume case depends on which is meant.
