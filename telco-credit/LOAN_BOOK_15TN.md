# A 15,000 bn Toman book — what it takes

Source: `test_all_1.xlsx` sheet `R3-1`, which gives the **median monthly
revenue of each band**, not just a threshold count. Revenue per KPI_v4.

| good months (of 4, over 170k) | subscribers | median monthly revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 4,424,088 | 397,193 |
| 3 | 2,247,487 | 259,529 |
| 2 | 2,345,583 | 190,601 |
| **2 or more** | **9,017,158** | |
| 1 | 3,368,783 | 130,624 |
| 0 | 28,348,244 | **3,401** |

Band 0 is 28.3 million subscribers whose median monthly revenue is 3,401
Toman. At a 50 pct stance over six months that sizes a ticket at 9,811 Toman,
which rounds to nothing on a 50,000 grid. **They are dormant, not
under-served, and no credit product reaches them.** The addressable base is
the 9.0 million with two or more good months, or 12.4 million counting one.

## At six instalments, 15,000 bn does not fit

Maximum affordable book, in billion Toman:

| instalment as a share of median monthly revenue | on 9.0M (2+ months) | on 12.4M (1+ months) |
|---|---|---|
| 30 pct | 4,478 | 5,152 |
| 40 pct | 6,156 | 7,167 |
| 50 pct | 7,613 | 8,792 |
| 60 pct | 9,408 | 10,923 |

Nothing reaches 15,000. The largest cell, 10,923 bn, needs an instalment
taking 60 pct of everything the subscriber generates in a month — and it
reaches that only by lending to people with a single good month in four.

The naive arithmetic shows why. 15,000 bn across the 3,000,000 target is
5,000,000 Toman each, an instalment of 866,667 a month against a top-band
median revenue of 397,193. That is 218 pct of what they generate. Spread over
all 9,017,158 eligible it is 1,663,495 each, an instalment of 288,339 — still
73 pct of the top band's median and 151 pct of the bottom band's.

## Two ways it does fit

**Extend the term.** The instalment is what the subscriber has to carry, and
it falls with the term:

| instalments | at 40 pct stance | at 50 pct stance |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 6,156 bn | 7,613 bn |
| 9 | 9,408 bn | 11,870 bn |
| **12** | **12,542 bn** | **15,676 bn** |
| 15 | 15,676 bn | 19,933 bn |

**12 instalments at a 50 pct stance absorbs 15,676 bn** on the 9.0 million
eligible, which is the first configuration that clears the target with room.

**Or recycle the capital.** If 15,000 bn is cumulative disbursement over six
months rather than capital at risk, lending 2,500 bn a month while earlier
cohorts repay needs a **peak of 8,750 bn** outstanding:

| month | disbursed | cumulative | outstanding |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2,500 | 2,500 | 2,500 |
| 2 | 2,500 | 5,000 | 4,583 |
| 3 | 2,500 | 7,500 | 6,250 |
| 4 | 2,500 | 10,000 | 7,500 |
| 5 | 2,500 | 12,500 | 8,333 |
| 6 | 2,500 | 15,000 | **8,750** |

That solves the *funding*, not the *affordability*: the same 9,017,158 people
still have to absorb 15,000 bn between them, so each takes about 1,663,495
Toman — either as one unaffordable loan, or as two sequential ones once the
first clears, which is a repeat-lending product and a different risk question.

## What this says

Six months and 15,000 bn are not compatible on this population. Twelve months
and 15,000 bn are, at a 50 pct stance, with the 9.0 million eligible
subscribers taking 500,000 to 2,250,000 Toman by band.

Three numbers to settle before anything is built on this:

1. **Is 15,000 bn capital at risk, or cumulative disbursement?** The answer
   changes the required funding from 15,000 bn to 8,750 bn and changes the
   product from a single loan to a repeat facility.
2. **What instalment share is acceptable?** 50 pct of monthly revenue is
   aggressive for a first cohort. Every table above is linear in it, so the
   choice is directly a choice of book size.
3. **Is the term fixed at six months?** It is the single most powerful lever
   here: holding everything else, 6 to 12 instalments doubles the book.
